Is Tankless Right for Your Home?

A tankless unit heats water as it flows, so it does not run out. It is not a fit for every house. A unit that cannot supply two showers at once, or a gas line that is too small, disappoints.

Tankless advantages

  • Continuous hot water
  • No standby heat loss
  • Compact, wall-mounted
  • Long service life with maintenance

Tankless trade-offs

  • Gas line or electrical upgrades may be needed
  • Venting must be exact
  • Needs periodic descaling
  • Higher purchase and install price

Gas or Electric Tankless

Gas units need a gas line sized for the burner load, a vent path and a condensate drain. Electric units need substantial electrical capacity, often more than an older panel can provide. We check both before we recommend a model.

Where the existing system is a tank, the swap is a larger job than replacing one tank with another. We explain the extras in the quote.

Flush and Descale Service

Minerals in water form scale inside the heat exchanger of a tankless unit, which reduces efficiency and can trigger error codes. A descaling flush circulates a mild acid through the unit and rinses it. Many manufacturers call for regular flushes, and the interval depends on your water.

We do not state a hardness figure for Maple Shade, because the 2024 water quality report from Maple Shade Utilities does not give one. A test strip on your own tap is the best guide, and we recommend a flush interval accordingly.

We check the error code first because many have a simple cause. If the exchanger itself has failed, replacement of the unit may be the sensible route.

Tankless in Maple Shade

Maple Shade has many apartments and townhomes from the 1960 to 1980 building period. Space is an advantage for tankless there, because a wall unit frees a closet. The limiting factor is usually the gas meter and the line, so we check capacity first.

The township buys part of its water from New Jersey American Water and treats its own well water for iron, per the 2024 report. Whatever the mix, an inlet screen and regular service protect the unit. We also work in Cherry Hill and Moorestown.

Installing Tankless in a Home With a Tank Today

Replacing a tank with a tankless unit usually means a new gas line or electrical circuit, a new vent and a condensate drain. The wall space is simpler than a closet, but the planning is longer. We survey the old setup and tell you what the conversion involves before we quote.

Some homes are better served by a tank. A very small household, a tight budget or limited gas capacity can all point that way, and we will tell you if so.
