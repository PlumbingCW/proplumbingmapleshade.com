Repairing a Dripping Outside Faucet, Step by Step

A drip from the hose bib is cheap to fix when the cause is a washer or a nut. It is expensive when it is a split pipe inside the wall.

By the Maple Shade Pro Plumbing team · Published November 12, 2026 · Plumbing guide for Maple Shade, NJ homeowners

The Short Answer

Key points

  • Where it drips tells you the cause. The spout points to a washer, the handle to packing and the wall to a bigger problem.
  • Tighten the packing nut a quarter turn before replacing parts.
  • Disconnect hoses and drain non-frost-proof faucets before winter.
  • Water inside the wall or basement means a split pipe, so call a plumber.
  • Replace a corroded or discontinued faucet instead of chasing parts.

Find Out Where It Drips

Look at where the water appears. A drip from the spout when the faucet is closed points to a worn washer or seat. A drip from around the handle points to a loose or worn packing nut. A drip or spray inside the wall, basement or crawlspace points to a different problem entirely.

Where it dripsLikely causeFix
From the spout, handle offWorn washer or damaged seatReplace washer, or the faucet
Around the handleLoose packing nut or worn packingTighten, or replace packing
At the wallLoose connection or damaged bodyReseal or replace
Inside the houseFrozen and split pipeCall a plumber, shut off the water

Tightening the Packing Nut

The packing nut sits behind the handle. If water seeps around the stem, turn the nut a quarter turn clockwise with a wrench. Do not overtighten, which can make the handle stiff. If it still leaks, the packing inside is worn.

To replace it, shut off the water supply, remove the handle and unscrew the packing nut. Wrap new packing string or install a new washer, then reassemble and test.

Replacing the Washer

1

Shut off the water

Close the indoor shutoff for the outside line, then open the faucet to drain it.

2

Remove the stem

Unscrew the packing nut and pull the stem out.

3

Swap the washer

Remove the screw at the stem end, replace the rubber washer with the same size.

4

Reassemble and test

Put it back together, open the shutoff slowly and check for drips.

Bring the old washer to the store to match it. A wrong size leaks or wears quickly.

Frost-Proof Faucets and Winter

A frost-proof hose bib has a long stem that puts the shutoff valve inside the heated space. Water in the pipe beyond it drains out, so it cannot freeze there. These faucets need to slope slightly downward to drain, and a hose left attached traps water and defeats the design.

Disconnect hoses in the fall. Shut off the indoor valve to older outdoor faucets and drain them. A spigot that was not shut off before a freeze can split inside the wall, and it shows as a leak in spring when the weather warms.

When to Call a Plumber

Call if you see water inside, if the faucet will not shut off or if the body is cracked. A broken pipe in a wall can leak quietly for weeks. If the basement ceiling near the faucet is stained, that is a sign to check.

For hidden leaks see leak detection. We repair faucets across Maple Shade and in Mount Laurel and Moorestown.

Parts and Tools to Gather

A basic repair needs an adjustable wrench, a screwdriver, packing string or washers in the right size and a small tub of plumber's grease. Take a photo of the faucet before you start, and bring the old part to the store.

Replacing the whole faucet requires soldering or threaded connections depending on the pipe, and that is often better left to a plumber.

Testing the Repair

After reassembly, open the water slowly and look for drips at the spout, the handle and the wall. Leave a dry paper towel under the faucet overnight. A damp towel in the morning means the repair is not done.

Check the basement or the wall behind the faucet a day later. A leak inside is easy to miss and easy to fix early.

A Small Upgrade That Helps

Consider adding a vacuum breaker to a hose bib, which prevents water from flowing backward into the house supply when pressure drops. Some outdoor faucets already include one. It protects drinking water from garden chemicals and is a cheap, simple part.

Replace the faucet entirely if the body is corroded or discontinued. Parts for old bibs are hard to find, and a new frost-proof model solves both problems.
