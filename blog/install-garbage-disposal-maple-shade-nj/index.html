What You Need to Install a Garbage Disposal

A disposal replaces a sink strainer, a drain connection and an electrical outlet. Each of the three can stop the job if it is not ready.

Talk to a plumber
(856) 250-1784
Call Now

By the Maple Shade Pro Plumbing team · Published November 12, 2026 · Plumbing guide for Maple Shade, NJ homeowners

The Short Answer

Key points

  • Check cabinet space, the drain layout and the electrical setup before you buy a disposal.
  • Knock out the dishwasher inlet plug if a dishwasher connects to the unit.
  • If there is no switch or outlet, wiring has to be added.
  • New Jersey requires licensed plumbers for plumbing work, and permits may apply.
  • Run water and test for leaks at the flange, the drain and the dishwasher hose.

Check Your Sink and Cabinet First

Disposals fit standard 3.5 to 4 inch sink drain openings, so most kitchen sinks work. A shallow cabinet or a sink with a deep basin can leave little room. Measure from the sink flange to the bottom of the cabinet, and check where the drain pipe and the dishwasher hose run.

A two-bowl sink with the dishwasher draining into the disposal is the classic setup. If you have a single bowl or a different drain layout, the plumbing connections change.

Parts and Tools

Basic hand toolsScrewdrivers, pliers, adjustable wrench

Look for a wall switch near the sink and an outlet under it. If neither exists, an electrician or a plumber with electrical authority has to add them, and that is a separate job.

How the Installation Works

1

Remove the old strainer

Disconnect the trap and unscrew the old sink flange assembly.

2

Mount the new flange

Seal it with putty, and attach the mounting ring from below.

3

Hang the disposal

Lift the unit onto the mount and lock it in place.

4

Connect drain and power

Attach the discharge tube, and the dishwasher hose, then power it.

Knock out the dishwasher inlet plug if a dishwasher connects to the disposal. Forgetting it blocks the dishwasher drain, which is one of the most common mistakes.

Local sourceNew Jersey's Uniform Construction Code plumbing subcode is based on the National Standard Plumbing Code, and electrical work has its own rules. Whether a disposal installation needs a permit depends on the work, so ask the Maple Shade Construction Office at 200 Stiles Avenue before you start.

When to Hire a Plumber

Hire help when the plumbing needs changing, when wiring has to be added or when the cabinet is tight. New Jersey requires plumbing work to be done by a licensed master plumber or authorized contractor, so a pro is the safe answer in most cases.

If you have an existing disposal that has failed, see garbage disposal repair. For a new fixture, plumbing installation covers the rest of the kitchen.

Noise, Power and Features

Larger motors grind more finely and often run quieter, and sound insulation varies by model. Continuous-feed disposals run while a switch is on, and batch-feed models run only with the stopper in place.

Think about how you cook and how many people use the sink. A small household can do well with a basic unit.

Common Install Problems

The usual problems are a leak at the flange from too little putty, a loose drain connection and a dishwasher that will not drain. Each is quick to fix if you find it during the test.

Tighten gradually and recheck after a day, since connections settle.

Costs You Might Not Expect

The disposal is the visible cost. The hidden costs are a new outlet or switch, replacing a corroded trap or drain pipe, and fixing a leaking sink flange. They are small compared with a flooded cabinet, and they are why a quote should list everything.

If the cabinet already holds a disposal that is leaking, the repair or replacement picture changes. See the repair page for the symptoms.

Septic, Condo and Rental Notes

Check your lease or condo rules before you install a disposal, since some forbid them. Septic systems can handle light use, but heavy use fills the tank faster.

If you rent, ask the landlord first. Unauthorized installs can cause disputes, and landlords sometimes prefer to choose the model.

Test It and Use It Well

Run cold water, turn on the unit and check for leaks at the flange, the drain connection and the dishwasher hose. Tighten any that seep. Test the dishwasher drain, too, since a missed plug shows up there.

Use cold water while grinding, and feed scraps in small amounts. Skip grease, bones and fibrous peels in quantity. We install disposals in Maple Shade and in Cinnaminson and Cherry Hill.
