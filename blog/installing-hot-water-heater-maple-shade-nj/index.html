Installing a Hot Water Heater: What the Job Really Involves

A water heater looks like a swap of one box for another. The details around the box are what make the job safe and legal.

Talk to a plumber
(856) 250-1784
Call Now

By the Maple Shade Pro Plumbing team · Published November 12, 2026 · Plumbing guide for Maple Shade, NJ homeowners

The Short Answer

Key points

  • A proper install includes venting or wiring, a relief valve with a safe discharge line, shutoffs and, where needed, an expansion tank.
  • Fill an electric tank completely before turning the power on, or the elements burn out.
  • New Jersey requires licensed plumbers for this work, and a permit may apply.
  • Measure the space and read the old data label before you buy a new tank.
  • Plan the replacement before the old tank fails, when you can still choose.

The Parts of an Installation

A tank is the center of the job, but the pieces around it do most of the safety work. The temperature and pressure relief valve protects against a dangerous buildup. The shutoff valves let you isolate the tank. The expansion tank, where needed, absorbs pressure when water heats.

For a gas unit, add the gas connection, the draft hood or direct vent and combustion air. For an electric unit, add the circuit, the breaker and the disconnect. Each part has code requirements, and a missing one is a callback waiting to happen.

Step by Step

1

Shut off and drain

Close the supply, turn off power or gas and drain the old tank to a drain or outdoors.

2

Disconnect and remove

Detach water, gas or electric and vent connections, then move the old tank out.

3

Set and connect

Place the new tank in the pan, connect water lines, add the expansion tank and relief line.

4

Vent, fill and test

Connect the vent, fill the tank, check for leaks, then restore power or gas and set the temperature.

Fill the tank completely before turning on an electric unit. A heating element that runs dry burns out in seconds, and it is one of the most common installation mistakes.

Measure the space and check the old tank's data label before shopping. Many of the surprises on installation day come from a new tank that is a bit larger than the old one.

Whether your job needs a permit depends on the work, so ask the Maple Shade Construction Office at 200 Stiles Avenue.

Why a Licensed Plumber Matters Here

A water heater stores a lot of energy. A poorly installed gas unit can leak carbon monoxide, and a missing relief valve can turn a thermostat failure into a rupture. Licensing law exists because the consequences of a mistake are serious.

A licensed plumber also knows when a permit and inspection are needed. Skipping them can create problems when you sell the home or file an insurance claim.

Choosing the Right Replacement Before the Old Tank Fails

Look at the label for capacity, fuel type and the manufacture date. Make a note of the venting layout and of any signs of corrosion on fittings. A tank near the end of its life gives you time to choose, and a failed one forces a quick decision.

A planned install also lets you ask about energy factor, recovery rate and a drain pan with a drain line. Those details do not matter on the day a tank fails, and they matter every day after.

Common Mistakes on Installation Day

The most frequent mistakes are skipping the pan and drain line, capping the relief valve line, forgetting to fill an electric tank before power and reusing corroded fittings. Each one is cheap to avoid and expensive to fix later.

A licensed plumber checks all of them as a matter of routine. If you hire anyone, ask them to describe their checklist in plain words.

What Your Quote Should Include

A clear quote lists the tank size and fuel, the venting work, the expansion tank, the relief line, shutoff valves, permit costs if any and the removal of the old tank. It states what is not included.

Quotes that look cheap sometimes leave out these items. Ask before you sign.

Aftercare in the First Week

Watch for drips at fittings for a few days, since connections can settle. Check the relief valve line for moisture. Set the thermostat at a safe level, and ask the installer to show you where the shutoffs are.

Plan an annual drain-down to remove sediment. We serve homes in Moorestown and Mount Laurel too, and the advice is the same. For a tank that has already failed, see water heater replacement.
