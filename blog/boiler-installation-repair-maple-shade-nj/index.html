Boiler Installation and Repair: What to Know First

A boiler fails in the cold months, which makes timing the biggest part of the decision. Here is how to tell a repair from a replacement.

Talk to a plumber
(856) 250-1784
Call Now

By the Maple Shade Pro Plumbing team · Published November 12, 2026 · Plumbing guide for Maple Shade, NJ homeowners

The Short Answer

Key points

  • Cold radiators, banging and water at the base are the main warning signs.
  • Failed circulators, zone valves and leaking fittings are usually repairs.
  • A cracked vessel or an old boiler with repeated failures may be better replaced.
  • A new boiler must be sized to the heat loss of the house.
  • Schedule service in the fall, not during the first cold snap.

Signs a Boiler Is Struggling

Cold radiators

Air in the lines, a failed circulator or a closed zone valve.

Banging or kettling

Air, low water or scale on the heat exchanger.

Water at the base

A leaking fitting, relief valve or the vessel itself.

Rising bills

The boiler is working harder for the same heat.

Some of these are fixes you can try, like bleeding a radiator. Others, like water at the base, need a plumber before the leak gets worse.

Repair or Replace?

SituationUsual answer
Failed circulator or zone valveRepair
Leaking fitting or relief valveRepair
Cracked heat exchanger or vessel leakReplace
Boiler more than about 20 years old with repeated repairsConsider replacing
Heating bills climbing and parts hard to findCompare repair with replacement

Age alone is not a reason. A well-kept boiler can run for decades. The question is the cost of repair compared with the age, the efficiency and how likely the next failure is.

What Installation Involves

A boiler install connects the supply and return piping, a gas or oil line, a vent and a drain. It includes an expansion tank, an air separator, a relief valve and a pressure gauge. The new boiler has to be sized to the heat loss of the house, not to the old boiler by default.

Fuel, venting and electrical work have their own rules, and heating contractors often do the burner side. We tell you honestly which parts we do and which need a specialist.

Local sourceThe township's 2006 master plan records large multifamily construction between 1960 and 1980. Many buildings from that period use central hydronic boilers, and aging circulators and zone valves are common failure points. If you manage one, schedule a pre-season check.

Preparing for Winter

  • Bleed radiators and check the pressure gauge
  • Look for stains or white crust at joints
  • Check that the relief valve line is clear
  • Replace worn thermostats if rooms are uneven
  • Schedule a service visit in the fall

A visit in October is easier than one in January. If the boiler fails in winter, call the 24/7 line, and we try to book the next available visit.

Sizing and Efficiency

A boiler that is too large cycles on and off often, which wastes fuel and wears parts. A proper heat loss calculation sizes the boiler to the house, and a modulating condensing unit can raise efficiency further when the system is designed for lower water temperatures.

Ask any installer how the boiler was sized and not only what brand it is.

Warranty and Records

Keep the installation date, the model and serial number and any service records. Manufacturers often require annual service to keep a warranty valid.

A short service log also helps us diagnose a problem quickly when the heat goes out.

Choosing Between Radiators and Baseboards Upgrades

If your house uses cast iron radiators, a new boiler can work with them at the right water temperature. Baseboard systems use less water volume and respond faster. Mixed systems need careful zoning.

A heating contractor can tell you whether upgrades to piping or controls make sense with a new boiler. We handle the piping and the water side, and we say so when a specialist is better.

Controls matter. A good thermostat setup and zone valves can cut fuel use as much as the boiler itself.

What Winter Emergencies Look Like

The classic emergency is a house with no heat in a freeze. If you have to leave the boiler off, open cabinet doors to let warm air reach pipes, let a faucet drip and keep the house as warm as you can with space heaters used safely.

If the cause is a leak, shut off the water feed to the boiler. Call the 24/7 line and describe what you see.

Related Plumbing Problems

Frozen or split pipes can look like heating problems. If water is escaping, see burst pipe repair. A leaking domestic hot water tank is a different job, covered by water heater repair.
