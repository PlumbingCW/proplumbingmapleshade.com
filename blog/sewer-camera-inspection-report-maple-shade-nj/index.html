How to Read a Sewer Camera Inspection Report

A camera report can read like a foreign language. These are the findings that matter and the questions to ask about each.

Talk to a plumber
(856) 250-1784
Call Now

By the Maple Shade Pro Plumbing team · Published November 12, 2026 · Plumbing guide for Maple Shade, NJ homeowners

The Short Answer

Key points

  • A good report lists pipe material, length inspected and each finding with its distance from the cleanout.
  • Roots, bellies, offsets, cracks, scale and collapse are the main findings, and each has a typical response.
  • Ask for the video and not only the notes.
  • Find out whether each defect is on your lateral or on the public main.
  • Not every finding is urgent, and a plan beats a panic.

What the Report Includes

A good report lists the date, the address, the pipe material and the length inspected. It includes a video file and notes on each finding with its distance from the cleanout. Photos or stills of defects are common.

Ask for the video, not just the notes. A defect described as minor in writing may look different on screen, and the video is what you share with a buyer or a contractor.

The Findings You Will See

FindingWhat it meansTypical response
RootsRoots entering a joint or crackCut out roots, consider lining or repair
Belly or sagA low spot holding waterMonitor, or dig to correct if severe
Offset jointPipe sections out of lineRepair if it catches debris or lets in soil
Crack or fractureA break in the pipe wallRepair or line the section
Scale or greaseBuildup narrowing the pipeClean or jet, see hydro jetting
CollapseThe pipe has failedDig and replace the section

Not every finding needs action now. A small offset with free-flowing water can be left alone and watched. A crack with roots or a belly that holds waste deserves attention.

Questions to Ask the Inspector

  • How far from the cleanout is each defect?
  • Is the defect on my lateral or on the public main?
  • What is the pipe material, and how old is it likely to be?
  • Is it urgent, or can it wait for a planned repair?
  • Can I see the footage at each marked point?

Those five questions turn a report into a plan. If the answer to the second is the public main, the township or its authority fixes it, and you only need to document it.

Local sourceThe 2006 Maple Shade master plan mentions a sewer-connection moratorium in 1988. It does not say what caused it. It does tell you the sewer system has been a planning concern, so a camera pass on an older lateral before buying or remodeling is a sensible step.

After You Read It

If the report is clean, keep the video with your home records. If it shows damage, get a written price for the repair before you commit. Options range from cleaning to lining to excavation, covered on our sewer line repair page.

When you are buying a home, a report with defects can support a request for a credit or repair. Your agent or attorney can use the footage. We inspect in Maple Shade and in Pennsauken and Moorestown.

What a Clean Report Looks Like

A clean report shows a continuous pipe, tight joints, no roots and free flow. Even then, note the pipe material. Clay and cast iron have a finite life, and a clean report today is not a promise for the next twenty years.

Keep the video, the date and the notes with your home records. A second inspection years later shows whether anything has changed.

Costs, Timing and Who Pays

An inspection is a planned service with a clear price, and repairs are priced separately. In a sale, buyers or sellers may share the cost by agreement.

If a defect is found in the lateral, the owner of the property is generally responsible. If it is in the public main, the township or its authority is.

Using the Report in a Home Sale

Buyers often ask for a sewer inspection in an older home, and sellers sometimes order one ahead of time. A clean report builds confidence. A report with defects opens a negotiation, and having the numbers ready makes it easier.

Your agent or attorney decides how to handle the findings. We provide the footage and a plain explanation.

Maintenance After the Report

If roots were found, ask about a yearly cleaning schedule. If grease was found, change habits and consider jetting. If the pipe is sound, note when it was inspected and plan another look in a few years.

Whatever the findings, keep the footage. It is the best baseline you will have.

A Camera Does Not See Everything

A camera shows the inside of the pipe it can reach. It cannot see past a full blockage or through a bad bend. A very small leak may not be visible, and a pipe can look fine and still be at the end of its life.

That is why we pair the camera with the history of the pipe and the symptoms you have had. See sewer inspection for what a visit includes.
