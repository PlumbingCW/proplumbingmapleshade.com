A Township With Its Own Shape

Cinnaminson Township covers about eight square miles in Burlington County and had 17,064 residents in the 2020 Census. It was incorporated in 1860 from Chester Township, which is now Maple Shade. Its areas include Bellview, East Riverton, New Albany, Parry, Taylor and Wrightsville.

Water and Sewer in Cinnaminson

New Jersey American Water supplies water in Cinnaminson, and the Cinnaminson Sewerage Authority, at 1621 Riverton Road, runs the public sewer. A problem in the street belongs to the utility or the authority. Everything past your shutoff or your property line is yours, and we handle that side.

When a drain backs up, we run a camera to see whether the clog is in your lateral or the public main. That one step saves a homeowner from paying for a repair that belongs to someone else.

Safety Steps

Before We Arrive: What to Do in Cinnaminson

Do these while you wait

  • Switch off the water heater if the tank is leaking.
  • Do not use drains that are backing up.
  • Move belongings off the floor and take photos for your insurer.

Questions to Ask Before You Replace Anything

A failing fixture does not always need replacing. We ask how old the unit is, how often it has been repaired and whether parts are still available. A water heater with a failed element on a young tank is a repair. A tank leaking from the shell is a replacement.

The same logic applies to supply lines and drains. One failed section is a repair, and several failures in a row point to a bigger fix. We describe the weakest points first, give you a clear price and let you decide. If a repair is the better value, we say so, even when replacement would be a larger job.

Leak Clues Worth Knowing

Some leaks announce themselves, and others hide. Watch for a water meter that moves when everything is off, a musty smell in a closet or basement, warped baseboards and a bill that climbs without a reason. Any of these is worth a call.

If you can find the meter, write down the reading at night and again in the morning with no water used. A change means water is leaving the pipes. Bring that note to the call, because it helps us choose the right method before we arrive. Our leak detection page explains each method.
