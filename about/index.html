Who We Are

A Plumbing Line Built for Emergencies

Maple Shade Pro Plumbing operates from 451 Route 38 in Maple Shade, NJ. We built the company around one idea, which is that a person with water on the floor wants a human on the phone, not a form to fill in.

That is why there is no online booking form on this site. You call, you describe the problem, and we tell you what to do while a plumber is on the way. The price is stated before work starts, and you decide whether to go ahead.

How We Work

Licensing, Permits and Code

We are licensed and insured. In New Jersey, plumbing work must be performed by a master plumber or an authorized plumbing contractor, under the State Board of Examiners of Master Plumbers within the Division of Consumer Affairs.

The New Jersey Uniform Construction Code plumbing subcode is based on the National Standard Plumbing Code, and that is the baseline for installs and repairs. Permit and inspection rules vary by job, so we point homeowners to the Maple Shade Construction Office at 200 Stiles Avenue for the current requirements.

Our Territory

Maple Shade and the Towns Around It

Maple Shade is a Burlington County township of about four square miles, and it borders Cinnaminson, Moorestown and Mount Laurel in Burlington County and Cherry Hill and Pennsauken in Camden County. We take calls from all five neighbors and from Maple Shade itself.

Each neighbor has its own water supplier, sewer authority and construction office, and the details are on the service area pages. Knowing who runs what on your street often saves a phone call, and we pass that along.

Principles

How We Decide What to Recommend

We recommend the least expensive repair that will hold, and we say so when it will not. A failing tank gets replaced, and a failed element gets repaired. A blocked drain gets cleaned first, and a camera comes in when it returns.

We also keep our scope honest. Drying a wet basement, repairing foundation cracks and large new-construction plumbing belong to other trades, and we point you to them instead of stretching.

Safety

Lines We Will Not Cross

If a caller smells gas, we tell them to leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 before anything else. If water is touching electrical equipment, we tell them to stay out of the room. A plumber on the way is never more important than the person on the phone.

We will not cap a relief valve, skip a permit that applies or sign off on work we did not do. Those shortcuts save minutes and cost homeowners years of trouble, and we would rather lose a job than take them.

If you are unsure whether something is an emergency, call and describe it. We would rather answer a question than miss a leak.

Limits

What We Will Not Promise

We will not quote a firm price for a hidden problem until someone has looked at it. We will not promise an exact arrival time over the phone, because traffic, weather and other emergencies change it. And we will tell you plainly when a problem belongs to the utility, your landlord or another trade.

If you are weighing a repair against a replacement, we explain both. Start with the full list of services or see the towns we serve.
