Burst Pipe: Do These Five Things First

A burst pipe is an emergency from the first second, and our line in Maple Shade is open 24/7 with same-day visits when the schedule allows. Shut the main water valve, open the lowest faucet to drain the lines and switch off the water heater if the tank is draining.

1

Shut the main

It is usually on the front wall of the basement or near the meter. Turn it clockwise until it stops.

2

Drain the lines

Open a faucet at the lowest level and one upstairs to relieve pressure.

3

Cut power if wet

If water reaches outlets or the panel, stay out and call.

4

Contain the water

Move belongings, mop or use towels, and bucket the drips.

5

Take photos

Capture the pipe and the damage for your insurer or landlord.

If the main shutoff will not turn, do not force it. Call us anyway, because a seized valve is a common reason a small burst becomes a large one.

Why Pipes Burst in New Jersey

Freezing is the best-known cause. Water expands as it freezes, and a pipe in an unheated wall, crawlspace or garage can split along its length. The leak often shows after the thaw, when ice melts and pressure returns.

Age and corrosion are the other big causes. Galvanized steel rusts from the inside, and old copper can pit. A pipe that has failed once in a building of that age often has weak neighbors.

How We Repair a Failed Pipe

A temporary fix

  • Rubber clamp or wrap holds pressure for a short time
  • Does not address corrosion upstream
  • May fail again under heat or pressure

A proper repair

  • Cut out the damaged section and replace it with new pipe
  • Use fittings matched to the existing material
  • Pressure test before the wall is closed

Copper repairs are usually soldered or use push fittings. PEX is joined with crimp or expansion rings. Galvanized lines are harder, because threaded joints are often corroded, and we may recommend repiping once we see how many sections are failing.

Burst Pipes in Maple Shade Homes

Maple Shade grew from 5,223 housing units in 1970 to 8,576 in 1980, per its 2006 master plan, so many buildings have supply lines that are four or five decades old. Apartments and townhouses from that era often have pipes inside shared walls, which means a burst can reach a neighbor. Tell your property manager at once if you rent.

The main shutoff is sometimes stuck after years of disuse. If yours does not move freely, mention it, since replacing it during the repair is a small job compared to a flood. We also serve Cinnaminson and Cherry Hill, where older homes have the same issue.

After the Repair: Drying and Documentation

Once the leak stops, the next job is drying. Wet drywall and insulation can grow mold in a day or two, so run fans and dehumidifiers and remove soaked materials. Plumbing is not water mitigation, but we tell you what we saw so a drying contractor can start quickly.

Keep photos, the bill and any damaged items for your insurer. Many policies cover sudden bursts and not slow leaks, so ask early.

Preventing the Next Burst

Insulate pipes in unheated spaces such as garages, crawlspaces and exterior walls. Let a faucet drip during a hard freeze, and open cabinet doors so warm air reaches pipes under sinks. Disconnect hoses and shut off outdoor lines before winter.

If you leave for a holiday, keep the heat on and know where the main shutoff is. A neighbor with the number of our 24/7 line is a good backup.

Burst Pipes in Apartments and Shared Walls

In a multifamily building, a burst in one unit can affect others. Tell the property manager immediately, and tell neighbors below you if water is coming through the ceiling. Shutting the unit valve, not the building main, is often possible and limits the disruption.

We document what failed and where, which helps the managers and insurers sort out responsibility.
