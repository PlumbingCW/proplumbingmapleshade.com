Repiping in Maple Shade, NJ

When pipes leak in more than one place, discolor your water or choke your pressure, patching stops working. A repipe replaces the failing lines.

Signs It Is Time to Repipe

Repeated leaks

A second or third leak in a few years points to a failing system, not bad luck.

Brown or rusty water

Galvanized steel corrodes from the inside, and rust reaches the tap.

Low pressure

Corrosion narrows old pipes until the flow drops.

Known problem material

Polybutylene and some old galvanized pipe have a poor record.

A single leak does not mean the house needs a repipe. We find how many sections are weak before we recommend a scope.

PEX, Copper and What Fits Your Home

MaterialStrengthsTrade-offs
PEXFlexible, quicker to install, resists freeze splits betterNot for outdoor sun exposure, needs proper fittings
CopperLong record, rigid, heat tolerantMore labor and material cost, can pit in some water
CPVCLow cost, common in some buildsMore brittle with age and heat

We explain which material suits your house and why. Local code and the Maple Shade Construction Office decide what is permitted, and permit rules vary by job.

What a Repipe Involves

1

Survey

We trace the existing lines and find access points.

2

Plan

We choose a scope, whole house or sections.

3

Replace

New lines go in and old lines are disconnected.

4

Test and patch

Pressure test, then drywall patches where access was needed.

A repipe takes planning. The water is off during parts of the work, and some drywall is opened. We describe the plan so you know what to expect, and we do not start without your approval.

Repiping Older Maple Shade Buildings

The township's 2006 master plan shows Maple Shade nearly doubled its housing units between 1970 and 1980. Buildings from those years are now 45 to 65 years old, which is the age where original copper and some galvanized lines show their limits. We look at what is behind your walls before saying you need a repipe.

The 2024 report from Maple Shade Utilities discusses its lead service line inventory. The service line is separate from the house piping we replace in a repipe, so ask the utility what yours is made of. We also repipe in Cherry Hill and Pennsauken homes of similar age.

Repipe or Repair?

A repair is the right call when the failure is isolated. If one section has failed and the rest tests sound, we fix that section and note what we saw. A burst pipe repair or leak detection visit is the way to learn how widespread the problem is.

When leaks keep appearing, a repipe costs less over time than repeated calls and repeated wall repairs. We show you the failing pipe so you can decide with the facts.

Planning Around a Repipe

Tell us who lives in the home and what the water schedule looks like. We schedule shutdowns so a household can still cook and wash up for most of the day. Move furniture away from the walls we will be opening and plan for a bit of dust.

After the work, drywall patches need finishing and paint. Some owners handle that through their own contractor, and we leave access holes clean and flat so the patching is easy.

Insurance note

Replacing worn pipes is usually maintenance and not covered, but damage from a sudden failure sometimes is. Ask your insurer.

Code referenceRepiping is regulated work under the Uniform Construction Code, whose plumbing subcode follows the National Standard Plumbing Code (N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15). Ask the Maple Shade Construction Office what permit and inspection steps apply to a whole-house job.
