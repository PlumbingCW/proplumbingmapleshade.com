What Century-Old Plumbing Can Hide

Houses from before 1940 may have galvanized steel supply lines, cast iron drains and later copper additions joined in awkward ways. Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside, which reduces pressure and discolors water. Cast iron drains roughen and crack, which invites roots.

Replacing everything is not always the answer. We start with the pressure, the leaks and a camera on the drain, and we describe the weakest points first. When whole runs are failing, repiping is the lasting fix. For a single failed run, a targeted repair works.

Historic-district rules may affect exterior changes but not interior plumbing in the same way. Ask the township what applies to your project before work starts, especially if a repair touches an exterior wall.

Safety Steps

Before We Arrive: Protecting an Older Home

Do these while you wait

  • Shut the main water valve and open a low faucet.
  • Stay away from wet outlets, old knob wiring and the panel.
  • Do not run drains that are backing up.
  • Photograph the damage, especially on plaster and woodwork.

Water, Sewer and the Township

Moorestown runs water and sewer through its Department of Public Works at 601 East Third Street, and its North Church Water Treatment Plant treats the supply. That makes the township the one to call about a problem in the street, and we handle everything past your shutoff.

Moorestown-Lenola is a census-designated place of 14,240 people, and Lenola sits in that area. We serve Lenola addresses as part of the same township.

Protecting Plaster, Wood Floors and Finishes

Older homes often have plaster walls, hardwood floors and built-in woodwork that water damages quickly. When a leak starts, shut off the water, then move rugs and furniture, lift loose items from floors and ventilate the room. Wet plaster can loosen and wood can cup within a day or two.

Tell a drying contractor about the leak as soon as the plumbing is stopped. We describe where the water came from and how long it likely ran, which helps decide what to dry and what to remove. Keep photos of every wet area, since insurers ask for them.

Gas, Electric and Mixed Systems in Older Houses

Older houses often carry a mix of systems added over decades, such as a gas water heater beside an electric range or a boiler that heats the house and the hot water. Each combination has its own failure points and safety rules.

Tell us what you have and where the shutoffs are. If you smell gas, leave the house and call the gas utility or 911 before calling us. For a boiler that heats radiators, see boiler repair, and for tanks, water heater repair.

Large Homes and Bigger Systems

Larger houses often have two water heaters, multiple bathrooms and long supply runs. That is where water heater installation details like recirculation and expansion tanks matter. Leaks in long runs are harder to find, and leak detection saves tearing into finished plaster.
