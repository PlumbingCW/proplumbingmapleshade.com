Sewer Backup: What to Do First

A sewer backup is an emergency because sewage is a health hazard and the damage grows by the minute. Our emergency sewer line repair line in Maple Shade is open 24/7, and we book same-day visits when we can. Stop using sinks, tubs, toilets and the washer, keep children and pets out of the area and avoid touching standing water.

If sewage has reached outlets or appliances, stay out of the room and call. Do not run a dishwasher or washer, since both push more water into a line that cannot take it.

Signs the Sewer Line Itself Is Damaged

Repeat backups

A line that clogs again and again is often cracked or sagging.

Gurgling and slow drains

Several fixtures slow at once suggest a main-line problem.

Sewage smell outdoors

An odor in the yard or basement points to a leak.

Sinking or soggy ground

A wet or settling patch along the line suggests a break.

A camera inspection shows the pipe. It finds cracks, root intrusion, offset joints and low spots where water and waste collect, which is why we start there.

Your Line and the Township Main

A property owner is generally responsible for the sewer lateral from the house to the connection with the public main. The township or its sewer authority is responsible for the main itself. The 2006 Maple Shade master plan mentions a 1988 sewer-connection moratorium, a reminder that the sewer system has been stretched before.

A camera shows which side the blockage is on. If it is past the connection, the township utility is the one to contact, and we will tell you so instead of selling a repair you do not need. Heavy rain can overload lines and cause backups that clear on their own, which is a good reason to document what happened.

Older Lines and Tree Roots

Houses built between 1960 and 1980, a period of heavy growth in Maple Shade, may still have the original sewer lateral. Older lateral materials, such as cast iron, can corrode, and joints in any older pipe invite roots. Mature trees along Route 38 side streets and Main Street lots are the usual suspects.

We also repair lines in nearby towns such as Pennsauken and Moorestown, where older neighborhoods have similar laterals. For cleaning only, see drain cleaning or hydro jetting.

Pipe Lining, Explained

Pipe lining, also called trenchless repair, slides a resin-soaked liner into the old pipe and cures it into a new pipe inside the old one. It needs access at one or both ends and a pipe that is still continuous. It suits cracks, small holes and root-prone joints.

Lining reduces digging, but it is not a magic fix. Offsets, collapsed sections and deep bellies still have to be dug, and a liner slightly reduces the pipe diameter. We show you on camera why we recommend one method over another.

Backups After Heavy Rain

Rain can cause backups in two ways. Groundwater can enter cracked lines and overload the pipe, or the public system can run full and push water back toward homes. A basement with a floor drain is the first place it shows.

Document what happened, take photos and tell the township utility if neighbors have the same problem. A camera pass afterward shows whether your lateral let water in. If it did, repair keeps the next storm from repeating the damage.
