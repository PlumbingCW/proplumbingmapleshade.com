What a Sewer Camera Inspection Shows

A small camera on a flexible cable travels through the sewer line and sends video to a monitor. We see the pipe material, joints, roots, cracks, offsets and low spots, and we note the distance from the cleanout. You can watch with us and keep the footage.

FindingWhat it means
Root intrusionRoots entering joints, usually in older clay or cast iron
Belly or sagA low spot that holds water and solids
Offset jointPipe sections out of line, often from soil movement
Crack or breakA path for roots in and wastewater out
Scale or greaseBuildup narrowing the pipe, see hydro jetting

When to Get an Inspection

Repeat clogs

A line that backs up more than once needs a look.

Before buying

A home inspection rarely covers the sewer lateral.

Before major work

Remodels and additions can stress old lines.

After a repair

Confirms the repair or cleaning worked.

An inspection is planned work, but a sewage backup is not. If sewage is rising now, see sewer line cleaning, and we can camera the line after it is open.

Buying a Home in Maple Shade

Maple Shade's homes are often decades old, where many owners have held their homes for years. Spending a modest sum to learn the lateral's condition can prevent a surprise repair after closing. Many laterals from the 1960 to 1980 building boom have never been inspected.

A camera cannot tell you who owns a defect, but it shows its location and type. Your agent or attorney can use the footage to negotiate a repair or a credit.

Your Lateral and the Township Main

The lateral from your house to the connection belongs to you, and the main in the street belongs to the township or its sewer authority. We mark where the camera stopped and which side a defect was on. The 2006 master plan mentions a 1988 sewer-connection moratorium and a need for storm drainage control, so the system's capacity has been a local topic for years.

We inspect lines in nearby towns too, including Pennsauken, where the Pennsauken Sewerage Authority runs the sewers, and Cinnaminson, served by the Cinnaminson Sewerage Authority.

After the Inspection

We tell you what we saw in plain words and show you the footage. If there is no problem, you get that answer. If there is, we describe the choices, which range from cleaning to lining to excavation, with a clear price before any work starts. See sewer line repair.

Reading the Report

After the visit, we describe each finding with a location and a distance from the cleanout. Plan on seeing the type of pipe, joints, any roots or cracks and whether water flows freely. Pipes made of clay, cast iron, orangeburg fiber or plastic each have their own known problems.

If you are selling, a clean report is a plus to share with buyers. If you are buying, the report gives you something concrete to negotiate with. In either case, it replaces guessing with a recording.
