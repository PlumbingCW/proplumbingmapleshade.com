A Faucet That Will Not Stop Running

A faucet or valve that will not shut off is an emergency, and our faucet repair line in Maple Shade is open 24/7 with same-day visits when the schedule allows. Close the small shutoff valve under the sink. If there is no valve, or it will not turn, close the main shutoff and open a lower faucet to drain the line.

A tub or shower valve with no shutoff of its own leaves you only the main. Do not wait if water is soaking a ceiling below.

Drips are small leaks with real costs. A faucet that drips once a second can waste thousands of gallons in a year, so a drip is worth fixing even if it is only annoying.

Tub Spouts, Showerheads and Hidden Valves

A shower that drips after you turn it off usually has a worn cartridge. A showerhead that leaks at the wall, or a spout that sprays behind the tub, may point to a failed connection inside the wall. We try a trim repair first and open the wall only when it is needed.

If a ceiling below the bathroom shows a stain, treat it as an active leak and see leak detection.

Repair or Replace the Faucet?

A faucet with a worn cartridge on a sound body is worth repairing. A corroded, pitted or discontinued faucet is often better replaced, since parts can be hard to find. We tell you which route is cheaper over time before quoting.

Replacing a faucet is also a good time to replace the supply lines and angle stops, which are cheap parts that fail in older homes.

Faucets in Older Maple Shade Homes

Much of Maple Shade was built between 1960 and 1980 per the township's 2006 master plan. Original under-sink valves from those years often seize, and a stuck valve turns a ten-minute faucet repair into a main-shutoff job. We replace failing angle stops during the visit so the next repair is simple.

Our technicians also repair faucets in Cinnaminson and Moorestown homes where fixtures are older.

Kitchen, Bath and Outdoor Faucets

Kitchen faucets with pull-down sprayers fail at the hose and the diverter. Bath faucets fail at the cartridge. Outdoor hose bibs fail at the stem packing, and in winter at the freeze point inside the wall.

If your outdoor faucet drips from the handle, tighten the packing nut first. If it drips from the spout, the washer is worn. A frost-proof model with a long stem prevents freeze damage, and we install those too.
