Overflowing Toilet: Stop the Water First

A toilet that is overflowing or leaking at the base is an emergency, and our toilet repair line in Maple Shade is open 24/7 with same-day visits when the schedule allows. Turn the oval handle on the small valve behind the toilet clockwise to stop the supply. If water is still rising, lift the tank lid and push the flapper down to stop the flow.

Do not flush again. Use towels for the floor and keep water away from outlets and any ceiling below.

Clogged Toilet: Plunge, Auger or Call

Use a flange plunger and give it a patient, steady push before reaching for chemicals. If the bowl rises again or other drains gurgle, the problem is past the toilet and in the branch or main line. In that case see drain cleaning, and stop flushing.

Toilets that clog repeatedly can have a partial blockage in the trap or a sewer line problem. A closet auger can clear the trap, and a camera finds what is deeper.

Rentals, Apartments and Older Buildings

About half of Maple Shade households rent, based on the 49.0 percent owner-occupied rate in the 2020 to 2024 Census estimates. If you rent, report a leaking toilet to your landlord or property manager right away, since a slow leak can reach the unit below. We work with property managers and can describe the cause and fix in writing for your records.

Toilets in buildings from the 1960 to 1980 building boom are often the original fixture or a replacement that is decades old. Replacing a worn toilet with a modern one can cut water use per flush, and it is a simple job when the flange and floor are sound. Planned installs belong with plumbing installation.

Toilet Installation and Replacement

When the porcelain is cracked, the toilet rocks beyond repair or the model is badly outdated, replacement is the better answer. We remove the old toilet, check the flange, set a new wax ring and test the flush. You choose the toilet or we help you pick a height and bowl shape that fits the bathroom.

We also repair toilets in nearby towns including Mount Laurel.

Choosing a New Toilet

Three measurements decide whether a toilet fits. The rough-in is the distance from the wall to the center of the drain, usually 12 inches. The bowl shape, round or elongated, affects the footprint. The height, standard or comfort, affects how easy it is to sit and stand.

Flush type matters too. Gravity toilets are simple and quiet to maintain, and pressure-assisted models flush harder and are noisier. Water use per flush is printed on the bowl or the box, and a lower number saves water over time.

Leaks Under the Toilet and Floor Damage

A toilet that leaks at the base for months can soften the subfloor around the flange. If the floor feels spongy near the toilet, tell us before we pull it. A damaged flange or rotted wood has to be repaired for the new seal to hold.

Look for a stain or soft spot in the ceiling below, too. A bathroom on the second floor can leak into a room beneath it without ever showing at the toilet itself.
